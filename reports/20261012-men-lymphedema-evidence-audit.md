## Evidence conflicts and overstatements

| Current passage or implication | Evidence finding | Required disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Men are broadly diagnosed and treated later than women | The named Journal of Lymphoedema source is not identified. The evidence located supports under-representation and delayed diagnosis in some male experiences, but not the page’s population-wide male-versus-female comparison. A separate primary-leg study actually found female sex associated with later diagnosis. | Remove the comparative claim unless its exact supporting study is supplied. Keep a cautious statement that men can experience delayed recognition. |
| Pelvic lymph-node dissection after prostate-cancer treatment causes lower-limb lymphoedema in 10–20% | A 2022 systematic review found 0–14% lower-limb and 0–1% genital lymphoedema after prostatectomy plus pelvic-node dissection. Reported rates were higher when pelvic radiotherapy followed node dissection, but definitions were inconsistent and risk of bias was high. | Replace the single range with a brief explanation that risk varies substantially by treatment combination and study definition; cite the review. |
| Genital lymphoedema after pelvic treatment is “not rare” | The same review found very different ranges by treatment combination: 0–1% after prostatectomy plus node dissection, 0–8% after pelvic-node irradiation, and 2–22% after node dissection followed by pelvic radiotherapy. | Remove the unqualified frequency label. Explain that it is recognised but reported rates vary. |
| Many men with unexplained post-treatment swelling “have lymphoedema — they just haven’t been told” | Persistent swelling after cancer treatment warrants assessment, but oedema has other possible causes, including venous, cardiac, renal, liver, thyroid, medication-related, infection and thrombotic causes. | Replace the implied diagnosis with differential-assessment wording. |
| A home Stemmer-sign check is “one of the most reliable early indicators” | The current primary study already used for the maintained Stemmer guide reported sensitivity 92% and specificity 57%, including false positives and abnormal imaging with a negative sign. | Do not present it as a reliable self-diagnostic test. Link to the corrected Stemmer guide and state that a positive or negative sign cannot establish or exclude lymphoedema alone. |
| Earlier treatment reverses tissue change, stage progression becomes more likely with delay, and early consultation can “change your outcome” | Early assessment is reasonable, but the page states deterministic progression and outcome effects without defining the population, treatment or endpoint. | Use neutral assessment language; remove outcome promises and coercive urgency. |
| LVA can “reduce the condition itself” and offers a route for people who want to go “beyond compression” | This frames surgery as a step beyond conservative care and implies disease modification without patient-selection or outcome limits. | Reframe treatment as individualised conservative and, for selected patients, surgical options. Preserve compression and specialist therapy as part of care rather than a lesser alternative. |

## Primary sources checked

1. Clinckaert A et al. Systematic review of lower-limb and genital lymphoedema after prostate-cancer treatment. The review reports treatment-specific prevalence ranges, high heterogeneity and high risk of bias: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36428759/>.
2. National Cancer Institute patient guidance: cancer or cancer treatment can disrupt lymphatic drainage; new swelling should be discussed with the treating team: <https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/lymphedema>.
3. National Cancer Institute oedema differential: swelling can have several non-lymphatic causes and may require blood tests or imaging according to symptoms: <https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/edema>.
4. Goss JA and Greene AK. Stemmer-sign clinical accuracy study: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31624689/>.
5. Cooper-Stanton G et al. Qualitative review of men’s experiences with chronic lymphoedema; useful for psychosocial and under-representation context, not a population-level male-versus-female diagnostic-delay estimate: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9755568/>.
