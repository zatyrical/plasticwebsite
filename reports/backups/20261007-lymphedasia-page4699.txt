<p>Lipedema and lymphedema are often confused because both can affect the limbs and cause visible enlargement. However, they are different conditions. Lipedema is primarily a painful disorder of abnormal fat distribution, usually affecting both legs and often sparing the feet. Lymphedema is swelling caused by impaired lymphatic drainage, which may involve one limb, both limbs, the trunk, face, breast or genital region depending on the cause.</p>

<div class="la-note"><p><strong>Singapore lipedema assessment:</strong> If your main concern is painful symmetrical leg enlargement, easy bruising, family history or uncertainty after vein treatment, see the focused guide to <a href="https://lymphedasia.com/lipedema-singapore/">lipedema diagnosis in Singapore</a>. This comparison page explains how lipedema differs from lymphedema and when lymphatic imaging may still be relevant.</p></div>

<p>Correct diagnosis matters because treatment plans differ. Some patients may have features of both lipedema and lymphedema, sometimes called lipo-lymphedema, especially when longstanding tissue enlargement begins to overload lymphatic drainage.</p>

<h2 id="key-differences-lipedema-lymphedema">Key differences between lipedema and lymphedema</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Distribution:</strong> Lipedema is often symmetrical and affects both legs; lymphedema may be one-sided or related to surgery, cancer treatment, infection or congenital lymphatic problems.</li>
<li><strong>Feet involvement:</strong> Lipedema often spares the feet, creating a cuff-like transition at the ankles; lymphedema commonly involves the foot or toes in lower-limb disease.</li>
<li><strong>Pain and bruising:</strong> Lipedema tissue is often tender and bruises easily; lymphedema may feel heavy, tight or swollen but is not always painful.</li>
<li><strong>Pitting and fibrosis:</strong> Lymphedema may start as pitting swelling and progress to skin thickening or fibrosis; lipedema is more related to abnormal fat and tenderness.</li>
<li><strong>Response to elevation:</strong> Simple fluid swelling may improve with elevation; established lymphedema or lipedema often improves incompletely.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="combined-lipedema-lymphedema">Can lipedema and lymphedema occur together?</h2>
<p>Yes. In some patients, longstanding lipedema can overload the lymphatic system and lead to secondary lymphatic dysfunction. This mixed pattern can be harder to manage and may require careful assessment of fat distribution, fluid swelling, skin changes, venous disease and lymphatic imaging.</p>
<h2 id="assessment-singapore">Assessment in Singapore</h2>
<p>Assessment may include medical history, cancer or surgical history, family history, limb examination, foot and toe signs, skin quality, venous assessment and measurements. In selected cases, lymphatic imaging such as ICG lymphography or lymphoscintigraphy may help determine whether lymphatic dysfunction is present.</p>
<h2 id="treatment-pathways">Treatment pathways</h2>
<p>Treatment depends on the dominant problem. Lymphedema care may include compression, skin care, exercise, manual lymphatic drainage, infection prevention and, in selected cases, lymphatic surgery such as LVA or lymph node transfer. Lipedema care may focus on symptom control, compression, activity, weight-stable health optimisation and, in selected cases, specialist discussion of surgical fat reduction. The right approach depends on diagnosis, stage, symptoms and patient goals.</p>
