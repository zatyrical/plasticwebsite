## Concrete observed gaps
Complete raw article retrieved (23,624 characters; modified 13 August 2026), with no in-body anchor links. The page names journals without identifying most studies. It includes a 30–50% psychological-distress estimate, a broad assertion that aircraft cabin pressure worsens swelling, an unqualified suggestion of greater and durable surgical quality-of-life benefit, and a lengthy first-person quotation attributed to Dr Sun. No recording, approved quotation or exact supporting citations were located in this investigation. This does not establish that the quote is false; provenance remains unverified.

## Source-to-claim check
All sources checked 9 October 2026. Primary studies only; generalisation limits matter.

|Existing claim|Named evidence inspected|Disposition for clinician review|
|---|---|---|
|Aircraft cabin pressure is a well-established reason for worsening swelling|Ferguson et al., J Clin Oncol, 1 March 2016; doi:10.1200/JCO.2015.61.5948. Prospective breast-cancer follow-up found no significant association between flight number/duration and arm-volume increases across 3,041 measurements. Abstract read at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26644530/?dopt=Abstract|Existing categorical wording is not justified by this cohort. Evidence concerns breast-cancer-treated patients and arm volume; it does not prove every patient with established lymphoedema can fly without risk.
