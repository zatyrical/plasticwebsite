## Observed problem

The previous article presented household heat, ice, cold baths and alternating temperature therapy as effective lymphedema treatments. It stated that heat encouraged lymph flow, sweating expelled excess fluid, cold reduced lymphedema swelling, and alternating both provided maximum relief. It also recommended heating pads while resting or sleeping, Epsom-salt baths and repeated contrast sessions. These claims were unsupported or more certain than the current evidence.

## Evidence reviewed

1. Hill JE, et al. *The effectiveness and safety of heat/cold therapy in adults with lymphoedema: systematic review*. Disability and Rehabilitation. 2024. PMID 37431170. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37431170/
   - 18 heterogeneous studies.
   - Tentative evidence of benefit from controlled heat protocols.
   - Only two cold-therapy studies.
   - Limited adverse-event reporting.
   - No specific clinical recommendation could be made; heat intended as lymphedema treatment should remain within methodologically robust research.
2. National Cancer Institute, *Lymphedema*, updated March 2024. https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/lymphedema
   - Advises avoiding extreme heat because high temperatures can worsen lymphedema.
   - Identifies pain/tenderness, redness, warmth, swelling and fever as cellulitis warning signs.
   - Lists compression, complete decongestive therapy, therapist-guided manual lymphatic drainage, exercise, skin care and selected surgery among management options.
3. International Society of Lymphology 2023 Consensus Document. PMID 39207406. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39207406/

## Implemented

- Replaced the unsupported premise with an answer-first evidence summary.
- Distinguished controlled experimental heat protocols from unsupervised home heat.
- Removed claims that sweating drains lymphedema, Epsom salts improve circulation, cold reduces lymphedema volume, or contrast therapy provides maximum relief.
- Removed the fixed repeated heat/cold timetable and home-treatment endorsement.
- Added burn/skin-safety precautions and cautions for impaired sensation, circulation and diabetes.
- Added cellulitis warning signs and an urgent-assessment route.
- Added contextual links to the cellulitis guide, treatment pathway and contact page.
- Added PubMed, NCI and ISL sources.
- Removed Wikipedia.
- Preserved the permalink and author.
