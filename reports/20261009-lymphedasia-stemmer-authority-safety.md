## Evidence check

The published copy had treated a positive Stemmer sign as unusually or highly specific, mapped positivity to lymphedema stages in absolute terms, and presented an unverified first-person quotation attributed to Dr Jeremy Sun.

Primary evidence does not support those absolutes:

- Goss and Greene's 2019 clinical study of 110 patients reported sensitivity of 92% but specificity of 57%, documented false positives, and found abnormal lymphatic function in some patients with a negative sign: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31624689/>.
- A separate 2019 comparative study found that classic clinical signs can be unreliable in a substantial proportion of patients and supported objective confirmation where needed: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31281100/>.
- The 2023 International Society of Lymphology consensus document was checked as the current primary consensus reference; the article's specific attribution to the consensus was not substantiated from the source inspected: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39207406/>.
