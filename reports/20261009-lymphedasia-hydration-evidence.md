# Lymphedasia hydration evidence correction

Date: 9 October 2026  
Live URL: https://lymphedasia.com/hydration-and-lymphedema/  
WordPress post: 3698

## Observed problem

The article repeatedly described hydration as a direct lymphedema treatment and claimed that extra water would improve lymph flow, reduce swelling, improve circulation, protect skin and enhance other treatments. It also advised a fixed 8–10 cups daily, described clear urine as the goal, treated caffeine as inherently dehydrating and linked to Wikipedia.

These statements conflicted with the 2023 International Society of Lymphology consensus, which states that restricted fluid intake has no demonstrated benefit for peripheral lymphedema, while not establishing extra water as a drainage treatment.

## Published corrections

- Retitled the article to **Hydration and Lymphedema: What Water Can and Cannot Do**.
- Added an answer-first statement: normal hydration supports general health, but extra water has not been shown to drain lymphedema or reduce limb swelling.
- Distinguished general hydration needs from treatment of impaired lymphatic transport.
- Removed the fixed 8–10 cup prescription, clear-urine target and categorical caffeine/alcohol claims.
- Replaced the claim that dehydration causes water retention and worsens lymphedema with a cautious explanation of general dehydration symptoms and evidence limits.
- Added individual safeguards for heart, kidney, liver and electrolyte conditions.
- Clarified that water intake does not replace compression, therapy, skin care or assessment.
- Added contextual treatment, cellulitis and private-consultation links.
- Added four evidence-led FAQs and direct ISL/NCI sources.
- Removed the Wikipedia link.
- Corrected the Rank Math title and description while preserving the permalink and focus keyword.
