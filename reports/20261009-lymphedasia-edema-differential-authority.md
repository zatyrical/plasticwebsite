- Purpose: remove unsupported diagnostic absolutes and medication/staging language while preserving a useful, cautious route from persistent swelling to clinical assessment.

## Discovery and evidence

The recent LVA decision-page candidates at `/can-lva-cure-lymphedema/` and `/when-is-lva-surgery-too-late/` were checked first. Both already qualify outcomes, avoid a calendar cutoff and base suitability on examination, imaging and usable lymphatic channels. They were closed without edits.

The older oedema-differential article had not received a recent content-authority review. It presented heterogeneous cardiac, renal, venous and medication-related swelling as one uniform comparator and included several unsafe absolutes:

- positive Stemmer sign from Stage 1 onward and sometimes Stage 0;
- positive Stemmer sign as specific to lymphedema and not produced by venous oedema;
- all general oedema remaining pitting;
- categorical statements that diuretics are ineffective or may worsen lymphedema, without accounting for comorbid systemic indications;
- a claim that Stage 1 treatment with compression/diuretics closes an early-intervention opportunity; and
- a claim that microsurgical options close by irreversible Stage 3.

Primary and consensus checks did not support those absolutes:

- The 2023 International Society of Lymphology consensus is the current broad diagnostic/treatment reference and explicitly preserves individual clinical considerations: <https://isl.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2024-11/THE-DIAGNOSIS-AND-TREATMENT-OF-PERIPHERAL-LYMPHEDEMA-2023-CONSENSUS-DOCUMENT-OF-THE-INTERNATIONAL-SOCIETY-OF-LYMPHOLOGY.pdf>.
- A 110-patient study reported a sensitive but only moderately specific Stemmer sign and documented false positives: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31624689/>.
- A separate comparative study found classic physical signs unreliable in a substantial proportion of patients: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31281100/>.
- Venous and lymphatic dysfunction can coexist as phlebolymphedema, rather than remaining categorically separate: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24527138/>.

## Published correction

Thirteen supported, match-once Gutenberg edits:

1. removed the unsupported opening assertion that persistent swelling meant the original diagnosis was wrong;
2. removed the universal low-protein/intact-lymphatic-system comparison;
3. removed categorical diuretic advice;
4. removed the absolute cardiac-oedema resolution contrast;
5. removed the unsafe medication-induced-oedema subsection;
6. removed the oversimplified self-diagnosis table and its false-specificity interpretation;
7. corrected `lateral` to `bilateral` in the symmetric-swelling sentence;
8. removed rigid elevation-to-stage mapping;
9. removed the claim that all general oedema remains pitting;
10. corrected `ski` to `skin` in the Stemmer test explanation;
11. removed the alarmist irreversible-stage and closed-window section;
12. replaced directive/brand-heavy medication advice with a factual link to the existing private-assessment page; and
13. removed three unsafe takeaway rows while retaining the general differential and reassessment takeaways.

No new treatment outcome, eligibility promise, price, credential or drug instruction was added.
